# Messaging, Kafka and asynchronous processing

---

<a id="prod-msg-001"></a>

<details>
<summary><strong>PROD-MSG-001. Kafka consumer lag is growing. What are the likely causes?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

Production may have increased, consumption slowed, errors/retries grew, a partition is hot, or the consumer is rebalancing.

#### Interview answer

I check lag per partition, input rate, processing rate, consumer errors, retry backlog, DLQ rate, rebalance frequency, pod restarts and downstream latency. Then I compare consumer concurrency, partition count, batch size, database/API pool usage and whether one key or tenant creates a hot partition.

#### Deep dive

Lag is a symptom, not a root cause. It can come from producer spikes, slow handlers, poison messages, schema errors, broker issues or downstream bottlenecks.

#### Production angle

Mitigation might be pausing non-critical consumers, throttling producers, reducing concurrency to protect a database, scaling safely or rolling back a bad consumer release.

#### Traps / caveats

- Total lag can hide one stuck partition.
- Adding consumers does not help if partition count or downstream capacity is the limit.

#### Optional Anki

Q: First Kafka lag breakdown?
A: Lag per partition plus input and processing rates.

</details>

<a id="prod-msg-002"></a>

<details>
<summary><strong>PROD-MSG-002. A consumer repeatedly reprocesses the same message. What do you check?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

Check processing exceptions, offset commits, crashes, rebalances, transaction boundaries and poison-message handling.

#### Interview answer

I inspect logs for the record's topic, partition, offset and key. Reprocessing usually means the consumer fails before committing the offset, crashes after side effects, is stuck on a poison message, exceeds `max.poll.interval.ms`, or is rebalanced before progress is committed. I also check manual acknowledgment and transaction behavior.

#### Deep dive

At-least-once consumption allows duplicates. The business handler must be idempotent for side effects like payments, emails and database writes.

#### Production angle

Mitigation can be sending the record to DLQ/quarantine, fixing deserialization, pausing the partition or rolling back the consumer.

#### Traps / caveats

- Do not commit offsets before durable processing unless data loss is acceptable.
- Do not retry a permanent poison message forever.

#### Optional Anki

Q: Why does at-least-once processing require idempotency?
A: A message can be processed more than once.

</details>

<a id="prod-msg-003"></a>

<details>
<summary><strong>PROD-MSG-003. A poison message blocks progress. How do you mitigate it?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

Classify the failure, stop infinite retry, quarantine or DLQ the record, and fix the handler or producer contract.

#### Interview answer

I identify the exact record, error type and whether the failure is permanent or transient. If it is permanent data or schema issue, I move it to DLQ/quarantine with metadata and commit past it according to policy. If it is a code bug, I may pause consumers, rollback or deploy a fix before replay.

#### Deep dive

Poison messages are especially harmful when ordering requires a partition to stop at the failing record.

#### Production angle

DLQ records need ownership, alerting and safe reprocessing runbooks; otherwise they become hidden data loss.

#### Traps / caveats

- Do not replay DLQ messages before fixing the cause.
- Do not drop messages without business owner approval.

#### Optional Anki

Q: What should a DLQ record include?
A: Original topic/partition/offset, key, payload reference and error metadata.

</details>

<a id="prod-msg-004"></a>

<details>
<summary><strong>PROD-MSG-004. A consumer group keeps rebalancing. What do you inspect?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

Inspect restarts, heartbeat/session issues, slow processing, poll interval violations, deployment churn and GC pauses.

#### Interview answer

I check consumer logs, rebalance metrics, pod restarts, GC pauses, CPU throttling, network disconnects, `max.poll.interval.ms` violations and handler duration. Long processing, slow downstream calls or large batches can prevent polling and trigger rebalances. Rolling deployments can also churn membership.

#### Deep dive

Frequent rebalances reduce throughput and can create duplicate processing because partitions keep moving.

#### Production angle

Mitigation may involve smaller batches, pausing slow paths, cooperative rebalancing, static membership, graceful shutdown or safer rollout settings.

#### Traps / caveats

- Raising timeouts can hide slow processing instead of fixing it.
- Rebalance storms often amplify lag and duplicate side effects.

#### Optional Anki

Q: Common application cause of consumer rebalances?
A: Processing takes too long between polls.

</details>

<a id="prod-msg-005"></a>

<details>
<summary><strong>PROD-MSG-005. Message processing is slow because of a downstream dependency. What options exist?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

Throttle consumers, pause partitions, reduce concurrency, add backoff, use DLQ/retry topics, or decouple the dependency.

#### Interview answer

I first protect the downstream: reduce consumer concurrency, pause or throttle consumption, enforce timeouts, open circuit breakers and avoid synchronized retries. For transient failures, delayed retry topics or backoff can help. For sustained issues, I may route to DLQ/quarantine, disable non-critical processing or redesign with buffering/outbox patterns.

#### Deep dive

Scaling consumers can worsen the downstream outage if every message calls the same database or API.

#### Production angle

The goal is controlled backlog growth rather than failing every message or overwhelming the dependency.

#### Traps / caveats

- Do not let Kafka concurrency bypass downstream rate limits.
- Do not pause forever without alerting and backlog ownership.

#### Optional Anki

Q: Why can scaling consumers be dangerous?
A: It can overload the downstream dependency further.

</details>

<a id="prod-msg-006"></a>

<details>
<summary><strong>PROD-MSG-006. Events are processed out of order. What assumptions do you verify?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

Verify ordering scope, key choice, partition count changes, retries, parallel processing and producer behavior.

#### Interview answer

I ask what ordering was actually guaranteed: per topic, partition, key, customer or aggregate. Kafka preserves order only within a partition, so I check partition keys, producer routing, partition count changes, multiple producers, retries, consumer concurrency, async handling and retry/DLQ reprocessing. I also check whether timestamps were incorrectly treated as ordering guarantees.

#### Deep dive

Out-of-order processing is often a design assumption failure, not a broker bug.

#### Production angle

Fixes may require per-aggregate keys, sequence numbers, idempotent state transitions or buffering until prerequisites arrive.

#### Traps / caveats

- Do not expect global ordering from Kafka topics with multiple partitions.
- Increasing partition count can change key-to-partition mapping.

#### Optional Anki

Q: What ordering does Kafka naturally preserve?
A: Order within a single partition.

</details>

<a id="prod-msg-007"></a>

<details>
<summary><strong>PROD-MSG-007. A dead-letter topic is growing quickly. How do you investigate?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

Sample records, group by error type, correlate with deployments/schema changes and classify transient versus permanent failures.

#### Interview answer

I inspect DLQ growth rate, affected topics, producers, consumers, error classes and sample payload metadata. A fast spike often points to a bad deploy, schema mismatch, deserialization issue or dependency outage. I check whether the consumer is sending recoverable transient failures to DLQ too early or retrying incorrectly.

#### Deep dive

DLQ growth should have owners and alerts. Otherwise business work silently stops.

#### Production angle

Mitigation can be rollback, pause consumer, disable bad producer, quarantine bad data or throttle until downstream recovers.

#### Traps / caveats

- Do not treat DLQ as success.
- Do not expose sensitive payloads while inspecting DLQ.

#### Optional Anki

Q: What does a sudden DLQ spike after deployment suggest?
A: Consumer bug, producer contract change or schema mismatch.

</details>

<a id="prod-msg-008"></a>

<details>
<summary><strong>PROD-MSG-008. A producer reports successful sends, but consumers do not see messages. What do you check?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

Check topic, partition, key, offset, consumer group, commits, ACLs, filters, lag, timestamps and transaction visibility.

#### Interview answer

I verify the producer wrote to the expected topic and cluster, and identify topic, partition, offset and key. Then I check consumer group ID, subscription, assignment, offset position, lag, ACLs, deserialization errors, consumer-side filtering and whether consumers read committed transactional records only. I also check if messages went to an unexpected partition or environment.

#### Deep dive

"Successful send" means broker acknowledgment according to producer settings, not that every consumer processed the record.

#### Production angle

Operational evidence should include Kafka coordinates so a record can be traced end to end.

#### Traps / caveats

- Do not reuse consumer group IDs accidentally.
- Do not confuse publishing with business processing.

#### Optional Anki

Q: What identifies a Kafka record location?
A: Topic, partition and offset.

</details>

<a id="prod-msg-009"></a>

<details>
<summary><strong>🧠 PROD-MSG-009. How do you safely replay or reprocess messages after fixing a bug?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

Use a runbook: define scope, backup offsets, ensure idempotency, dry-run if possible, throttle and validate results.

#### Interview answer

I identify exact topics, partitions, offsets or timestamps to replay and the business records affected. Before changing offsets or re-emitting records, I verify handlers are idempotent and old events still match current schema and business rules. I back up current offsets, stop or coordinate consumers, replay with throttling, monitor side effects and reconcile results.

#### Deep dive

Replay is not rollback. It can duplicate writes, resend notifications or overwrite newer state if handlers are not designed for it.

#### Production angle

High-risk reprocessing needs approval, audit trail, metrics, rollback/repair plan and business validation.

#### Traps / caveats

- Do not replay one month of events at full speed into production dependencies.
- Do not assume old event payloads are still compatible.

#### Optional Anki

Q: What is the main safety requirement for replay?
A: Idempotent processing and bounded replay scope.

</details>
