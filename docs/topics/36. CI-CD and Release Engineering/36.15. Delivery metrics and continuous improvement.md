# Delivery metrics and continuous improvement

---

<a id="cicd-met-001"></a>

<details>
<summary><strong>CICD-MET-001. What are DORA metrics, and why are they useful?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

DORA metrics measure software delivery throughput and instability so teams can improve delivery performance.

#### Interview answer

The current DORA delivery metrics include change lead time, deployment frequency, failed deployment recovery time, change failure rate and deployment rework rate. Older interview material often mentions the original four metrics, but the intent is the same: measure how quickly and safely changes reach production.

#### Deep dive

They are useful because they focus on outcomes of the delivery system, not individual developer activity. They should be interpreted per service or team context.

#### Production angle

DORA metrics can reveal whether a team ships in small safe batches or suffers from slow, risky releases.

#### Traps / caveats

- Do not use DORA metrics to rank individual developers.
- Metrics should guide improvement, not become vanity targets.

#### Optional Anki

Q: What do DORA metrics measure?
A: Throughput and instability of the software delivery process.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>CICD-MET-002. How do deployment frequency and lead time for changes measure delivery throughput?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

Deployment frequency shows how often changes reach production; lead time shows how long a change takes to get there.

#### Interview answer

High deployment frequency with low lead time usually means small batches, fast feedback and low release friction. Low frequency and long lead time often indicate bottlenecks in review, CI, testing, approvals or release coordination.

#### Deep dive

These metrics should be measured for a specific service or value stream. Aggregating very different systems can mislead.

#### Production angle

When lead time is high, urgent security fixes and production bug fixes also take longer.

#### Traps / caveats

- More deployments are not automatically better if change failure rises.
- Commit count is not a useful substitute for lead time.

#### Optional Anki

Q: What does lead time for changes measure?
A: Time from committed change to production deployment.

</details>

<a id="cicd-met-003"></a>

<details>
<summary><strong>CICD-MET-003. How do change failure rate, failed deployment recovery time and deployment rework rate measure release instability?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

They show how often deployments cause trouble, how fast the team recovers and how much unplanned rework incidents create.

#### Interview answer

Change failure rate measures the share of deployments that require intervention. Failed deployment recovery time measures how long recovery takes after a failed deployment. Deployment rework rate captures unplanned deployments caused by production incidents.

#### Deep dive

Together, they balance throughput metrics. A team deploying often but constantly hotfixing is not healthy.

#### Production angle

High instability points to weak testing, large batches, poor observability, risky migrations or fragile rollback.

#### Traps / caveats

- Definitions must be consistent across measurements.
- A rollback, hotfix or feature-flag disable may all count as intervention depending on policy.

#### Optional Anki

Q: What does change failure rate measure?
A: The share of deployments that require intervention after release.

</details>

<a id="cicd-met-004"></a>

<details>
<summary><strong>🧠 CICD-MET-004. What metrics can reveal that a pipeline is hurting developer productivity?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

Look at queue time, build duration, failure noise, reruns, flaky tests, review delays and time to restore main.

#### Interview answer

A painful pipeline shows long feedback cycles, many reruns, flaky failures, high queue time, repeated dependency downloads, low cache hit quality, frequent main-branch breakage and developers waiting instead of coding.

#### Deep dive

Developer surveys and qualitative feedback matter too. Metrics explain symptoms, but engineers can describe where the workflow hurts.

#### Production angle

Poor developer experience pushes teams toward larger batches and fewer releases, which increases production risk.

#### Traps / caveats

- Optimizing developer speed by removing safety checks can backfire.
- Queue time and failure rate should be segmented by job type.

#### Optional Anki

Q: Name a CI productivity metric.
A: Pipeline duration, queue time, rerun rate or flaky test rate.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>CICD-MET-005. Why can optimizing only for deployment frequency be harmful?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

It can encourage more deployments without improving safety, value or recovery.

#### Interview answer

Deployment frequency is useful only with quality and stability metrics. If a team chases frequency alone, it may split meaningless changes, ship low-value work, ignore failures or create constant operational noise.

#### Deep dive

Good delivery metrics have tension: throughput should improve without increasing instability or harming user outcomes.

#### Production angle

Frequent broken releases reduce trust in both the team and the deployment process.

#### Traps / caveats

- A service can deploy daily and still deliver little user value.
- Compare trends within context, not arbitrary industry targets.

#### Optional Anki

Q: Why not optimize only deployment frequency?
A: It ignores quality, value and reliability.

</details>

<a id="cicd-met-006"></a>

<details>
<summary><strong>🧠 CICD-MET-006. How should a team use CI/CD metrics without turning them into vanity metrics?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

Use them to find bottlenecks and guide improvement, not to punish or rank people.

#### Interview answer

The team should define metrics clearly, review trends, connect them to specific process problems and choose improvement experiments. Metrics should be combined with incident reviews, developer feedback and customer impact.

#### Deep dive

Goodhart's law applies: when a metric becomes the only goal, people optimize the number instead of the system.

#### Production angle

Healthy metric use improves release safety and speed; unhealthy metric use creates gaming and mistrust.

#### Traps / caveats

- Do not compare unrelated services without context.
- Do not hide bad reliability behind high throughput.

#### Optional Anki

Q: What is the safe way to use CI/CD metrics?
A: As team-level improvement signals with context and balanced trade-offs.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>🧠 CICD-MET-007. How would you diagnose a team with low deployment frequency but high production incident rate?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

Look for large batches, weak tests, manual releases, poor observability, fragile dependencies and risky data changes.

#### Interview answer

Low deployment frequency plus high incident rate usually means releases are large and risky. I would inspect lead time, batch size, flaky tests, release approvals, rollback history, migration failures, incident causes and whether the team can safely deploy small fixes.

#### Deep dive

The fix is usually not "deploy more" immediately. First improve CI trust, release slicing, contract safety, observability and recovery.

#### Production angle

The team needs to reduce release risk while increasing confidence and reducing batch size.

#### Traps / caveats

- Low frequency may be a symptom of fear caused by previous incidents.
- More automation without better quality gates may increase incidents.

#### Optional Anki

Q: What does low deployment frequency plus high incident rate suggest?
A: Large risky batches and weak release confidence.

</details>
