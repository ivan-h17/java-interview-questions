# Security, privacy and compliance

---

<a id="eda-sec-001"></a>

<details>
<summary><strong>EDA-SEC-001. How should publishing and consuming permissions be controlled?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

Use least-privilege identities and authorize producers and consumers per topic, queue, event type, or tenant as appropriate.

#### Interview answer

Services should authenticate to the broker with service identities. Producers should only publish events they own, and consumers should only subscribe to events they are allowed to process. Permissions should be managed as infrastructure and reviewed.

#### Deep dive

Authorization can be broker-level, platform-level, or application-level. Sensitive domains may also require payload-level controls and consumer approval before access.

#### Production angle

Audit publish/subscribe permissions, rotate credentials, and alert on unexpected producers or consumers.

#### Traps / caveats

- Internal events still need authorization.
- Broad wildcard permissions make incidents worse.
- Access to a topic may expose historical retained data.

#### Optional Anki

Q: Security principle for event access?
A: Least privilege for producers and consumers.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>EDA-SEC-002. Why is putting PII or secrets into events risky?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

Events are often retained, copied, replayed, logged, and consumed by many systems.

#### Interview answer

A password, token, full card number, or unnecessary personal data in an event can spread into brokers, logs, DLQs, data lakes, backups, and third-party consumers. Removing it later is difficult.

#### Deep dive

Events tend to have longer and wider lifetimes than request payloads. Data minimization matters: include only what consumers need, classify fields, and prefer references or tokenized values for sensitive data.

#### Production angle

Treat event schemas as data contracts with privacy review, retention rules, encryption policy, and redaction requirements.

#### Traps / caveats

- DLQs and logs can leak event payloads.
- "Internal topic" is not a privacy control by itself.
- Replays can re-expose data long after collection.

#### Optional Anki

Q: Why is PII risky in events?
A: Events are retained, copied, replayed, and widely consumed.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>EDA-SEC-003. How should encryption, tenant isolation and data classification be handled for event payloads?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

Encrypt in transit and at rest, classify event data, and isolate tenants through topics, keys, ACLs, or payload design.

#### Interview answer

Event platforms should use TLS and encrypted storage where required. Schemas should mark sensitive fields. Multi-tenant systems must prevent one tenant's events from being visible to another through routing, authorization, or shared consumer mistakes.

#### Deep dive

Isolation can be physical, logical, or application-enforced. The right choice depends on compliance, blast radius, cost, and operational complexity.

#### Production angle

Include event payloads in data classification, DLP checks, retention policy, and incident response plans.

#### Traps / caveats

- Encryption does not solve over-sharing with authorized consumers.
- Tenant ID must not be trusted blindly from unvalidated payloads.
- Field-level encryption complicates search, filtering, and debugging.

#### Optional Anki

Q: What does data classification add to event design?
A: It identifies sensitivity, retention, access, and handling requirements.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>🧠 EDA-SEC-004. How can audit requirements, data minimization and privacy deletion conflict with immutable event logs?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

Audit may require preserving facts, while privacy rules may require minimizing or deleting personal data.

#### Interview answer

Immutable logs are attractive for audit, but storing raw personal data forever can conflict with deletion or minimization requirements. The design may need pseudonymization, encryption with key deletion, separate PII stores, redaction events, or retention limits.

#### Deep dive

The answer is domain-specific and often legal/compliance-driven. A financial ledger may need immutable facts, but not necessarily full personal details inside every event.

#### Production angle

Design privacy before publishing: classify fields, keep PII out of durable streams when possible, and document deletion/anonymization behavior.

#### Traps / caveats

- "Immutable" is not a legal defense by itself.
- Deleting keys can make data unreadable but affects audit and replay.
- Corrective or redaction events do not erase already copied data elsewhere.

#### Optional Anki

Q: How reduce privacy risk in immutable logs?
A: Minimize PII, pseudonymize, encrypt carefully, and define retention/deletion policy.

</details>
