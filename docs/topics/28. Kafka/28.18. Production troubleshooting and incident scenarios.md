# Production troubleshooting and incident scenarios

---

<a id="kf-proddbg-001"></a>

<details>
<summary><strong>KF-PRODDBG-001. How would you investigate growing consumer lag?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

Check whether production increased, consumption slowed, errors/retries grew, or one partition became hot.

#### Interview answer

I would inspect lag per partition, input rate, processing rate, consumer errors, retry/DLQ metrics, recent deploys, rebalances and downstream dependencies. Then I would compare consumer processing time with database/API latency and thread/connection pool saturation.

#### Deep dive

Lag is a symptom. The root cause may be producer spike, slow database, poison message, rebalance storm, hot partition, broker issue or bad deployment.

#### Production angle

Mitigate by pausing non-critical work, scaling safely, reducing downstream pressure or rolling back a bad deploy.

#### Traps / caveats

- Total lag can hide one stuck partition.
- Adding consumers does not help if partition count or DB capacity is the bottleneck.

#### Optional Anki

Q: First lag check?
A: Per-partition lag plus processing and error rates.

</details>

<a id="kf-proddbg-002"></a>

<details>
<summary><strong>KF-PRODDBG-002. How would you investigate frequent consumer rebalances?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

Look for restarts, poll interval violations, heartbeat/session issues, deployment churn and slow processing.

#### Interview answer

I would inspect consumer logs, rebalance metrics, pod restarts, GC pauses, CPU, network disconnects and `max.poll.interval.ms` errors. Then I would check whether processing batches exceed poll interval or whether rolling deploys restart too many consumers at once.

#### Deep dive

Rebalances often come from consumers that are alive but not polling because they are blocked on slow dependencies or large batches.

#### Production angle

Mitigate with smaller batches, bounded processing, cooperative assignment, static membership, graceful shutdown and safer deploy rollout.

#### Traps / caveats

- Raising timeouts may only hide blocked processing.
- Rebalance storms can create duplicate side effects.

#### Optional Anki

Q: Common application cause of rebalances?
A: Processing too long between polls.

</details>

<a id="kf-proddbg-003"></a>

<details>
<summary><strong>KF-PRODDBG-003. How would you investigate messages not being consumed?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

Check topic existence, consumer group, offsets, assignment, filters, errors, ACLs and whether records are in expected partitions.

#### Interview answer

I would verify producers are writing to the expected topic, inspect consumer group membership and lag, confirm group ID, check listener subscription, ACL/auth errors, deserialization failures, paused partitions and offset reset behavior.

#### Deep dive

"Not consumed" may mean no records produced, wrong topic, wrong group, records already committed, consumer filtering them out, or the listener failing before business code.

#### Production angle

Use Kafka coordinates: topic, partition, offset, key and timestamp. Check logs with correlation ID where possible.

#### Traps / caveats

- A reused group ID may have already committed past the records.
- Deserialization errors can prevent listener execution.

#### Optional Anki

Q: What identity controls consumer progress?
A: Consumer group ID.

</details>

<a id="kf-proddbg-004"></a>

<details>
<summary><strong>KF-PRODDBG-004. How would you investigate duplicate or missing message processing?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

Compare Kafka records, offset commits and business side effects to determine whether the issue is delivery, processing or idempotency.

#### Interview answer

For duplicates, check retries, rebalances, crashes after processing before commit, replay, producer duplicate events and missing idempotency. For missing processing, check commits before processing, DLQ, filters, offset resets, retention expiration and failed side effects.

#### Deep dive

Duplicate Kafka delivery and duplicate business event are different. Missing business effects can happen even if Kafka consumption succeeded.

#### Production angle

Use event IDs and business IDs to trace through logs, DB tables and consumer offsets.

#### Traps / caveats

- Offset committed does not prove business side effect succeeded.
- Duplicate processing is expected under at-least-once semantics.

#### Optional Anki

Q: Main protection against duplicate side effects?
A: Idempotent consumer logic.

</details>

<a id="kf-proddbg-005"></a>

<details>
<summary><strong>KF-PRODDBG-005. How would you investigate producer timeouts or high producer latency?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

Check broker health, metadata, acknowledgments, retries, batch settings, network, message size and producer buffer pressure.

#### Interview answer

I would look at producer request latency, retry rate, error classes, buffer available bytes, record queue time and recent broker leader changes. Then check under-min-ISR, network issues, large messages, compression CPU and `acks`/timeout settings.

#### Deep dive

Producer latency may be caused by Kafka or by application threads blocking on sends. Distinguish async send latency from request-path latency.

#### Production angle

Mitigate by failing fast, using Outbox for critical events, rolling back bad payload changes or coordinating with platform on broker health.

#### Traps / caveats

- Increasing timeout can increase request thread exhaustion.
- `acks=0` may hide errors by losing durability.

#### Optional Anki

Q: Producer timeout can indicate what broker condition?
A: Unavailable leader or insufficient ISR, among others.

</details>

<a id="kf-proddbg-006"></a>

<details>
<summary><strong>KF-PRODDBG-006. How would you investigate under-replicated partitions, offline partitions or broker disk pressure?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

Check broker availability, disk usage/I/O, network, replica lag, partition leadership and recent reassignment or traffic changes.

#### Interview answer

Under-replication means followers are behind or unavailable. Offline partitions mean no leader is available. Disk pressure may come from retention, traffic spikes, too many partitions, large messages or failed cleanup. I would involve the platform team quickly.

#### Deep dive

These issues can affect producer success and consumer availability. Under-min-ISR can cause writes to fail with strong durability settings.

#### Production angle

Application mitigation may include pausing non-critical producers, reducing traffic, or enabling graceful degradation while platform restores cluster health.

#### Traps / caveats

- Deleting topics under pressure without impact review can cause data loss.
- Disk pressure can worsen during consumer replay or reassignment.

#### Optional Anki

Q: What does an offline partition mean?
A: No available leader for that partition.

</details>

<a id="kf-proddbg-007"></a>

<details>
<summary><strong>KF-PRODDBG-007. How would you investigate a poison pill or fast-growing DLQ?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

Classify failures by error type, deployment time, schema version, producer, topic and payload pattern.

#### Interview answer

I would sample DLQ records, inspect error metadata, compare with recent producer/consumer deploys, check schema compatibility and identify whether failures are permanent data issues or transient dependency failures. If one record blocks a partition, decide on DLQ/quarantine policy.

#### Deep dive

A fast-growing DLQ after deployment usually indicates consumer bug, schema mismatch or changed producer behavior. A slow trickle may be data quality.

#### Production angle

Stop the bleeding first: rollback, pause consumer, disable bad producer or route to quarantine.

#### Traps / caveats

- Replaying DLQ before fixing the cause creates a loop.
- DLQ records may contain sensitive data.

#### Optional Anki

Q: First step for fast-growing DLQ?
A: Classify failures and correlate with deployments/schema changes.

</details>

<a id="kf-proddbg-008"></a>

<details>
<summary><strong>KF-PRODDBG-008. How would you investigate and fix a hot partition?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

Find skewed partitions, identify hot keys, then adjust keying, routing or workload design.

#### Interview answer

I would compare per-partition bytes, records and lag. Then inspect keys for one tenant, customer or null key dominating traffic. Fixes include better partition keys, composite sharding, separate topic for heavy tenants, reducing ordering scope or changing consumer design.

#### Deep dive

Adding consumers does not fix one hot partition. Increasing partitions may not fix one hot key and can break key mapping assumptions.

#### Production angle

Short-term mitigation may be throttling the heavy producer or isolating traffic. Long-term fix is usually key or domain design.

#### Traps / caveats

- Sharding a key can break per-key order.
- Averages hide skew.

#### Optional Anki

Q: What identifies a hot partition?
A: Per-partition traffic or lag much higher than others.

</details>

<a id="kf-proddbg-009"></a>

<details>
<summary><strong>KF-PRODDBG-009. How would you investigate a slow database or external API causing consumer lag?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

Correlate consumer processing time and lag with downstream latency, errors, pool usage and rate limits.

#### Interview answer

I would check DB query latency, locks, connection pool exhaustion, API response times, timeout/retry rates and circuit breaker state. Then compare with consumer throughput and batch size.

#### Deep dive

Consumers can amplify downstream incidents through concurrency and retries. A slow DB can cause long polls, rebalances and duplicate processing.

#### Production angle

Mitigate with pause/resume, reduced concurrency, backoff, circuit breakers, rate limiting or temporarily disabling non-critical consumers.

#### Traps / caveats

- Scaling consumers can overload the dependency further.
- Retrying every failed message immediately can create a retry storm.

#### Optional Anki

Q: What should Kafka lag be correlated with?
A: Downstream dependency latency and capacity metrics.

</details>

<a id="kf-proddbg-010"></a>

<details>
<summary><strong>🧠 KF-PRODDBG-010. How would you safely pause, throttle, replay or reset offsets for a consumer group?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

Understand business impact, stop or coordinate consumers, choose exact partitions/offsets, and make processing idempotent before replay.

#### Interview answer

For pause/throttle, use application controls or `pause()`/reduced concurrency to slow consumption. For replay/reset, identify topic, group, partitions, target offsets/timestamps and expected duplicate side effects. Stop consumers or coordinate ownership before changing offsets.

#### Deep dive

Offset reset is dangerous because it changes what the group will process next. Reprocessing old records can duplicate emails, payments, writes or state transitions unless handlers are idempotent.

#### Production angle

Use a runbook with approval, dry-run, backup of current offsets, monitoring and rollback plan.

#### Traps / caveats

- Resetting offsets does not undo side effects already performed.
- Replaying old events can violate current schema or business assumptions.

#### Optional Anki

Q: Why is offset reset risky?
A: It can reprocess or skip records with business side effects.

</details>

<a id="kf-proddbg-011"></a>

<details>
<summary><strong>KF-PRODDBG-011. How would you handle Kafka being temporarily unavailable in a Java application?</strong></summary>

#### Short answer

Use bounded timeouts, retries, graceful degradation and durable fallback such as Outbox for critical events.

#### Interview answer

For producers, decide whether the request should fail, degrade or write to Outbox for later publication. For consumers, expect polling errors and resume when Kafka recovers. Avoid infinite blocking on request threads. Surface health carefully so one Kafka issue does not unnecessarily kill the whole service unless Kafka is critical.

#### Deep dive

The right behavior depends on event criticality. Losing audit or payment events is not acceptable; delaying analytics may be fine.

#### Production angle

Expose publish failure metrics, outbox backlog and circuit-breaker state. Alert on sustained inability to publish critical events.

#### Traps / caveats

- Fire-and-forget producers can silently lose events.
- Marking the whole app down for optional Kafka flows can cause avoidable outages.

#### Optional Anki

Q: Reliable fallback for critical publishes during Kafka outage?
A: Transactional Outbox.

</details>
